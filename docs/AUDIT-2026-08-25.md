# Audit, 2026-08-25: does nameproof already have name stats, and is the finder deep?

Dylan asked two questions, verbatim: *"si tu souhaite ameliorer le finder des noms si c'est pas
deja en place... meme pour les noms je sais pas si on avait fait des stats."* Both are audit
questions before they are build questions, so this document answers them first and ranks the
gaps second. Every claim below carries the file and the measurement that produced it.

## Short answers

**Do we have stats?** Yes, and they were written before this session: `nameproof/corpus.py` (132
lines) plus the `market` command measure five conventions of a market and `score --market` turns
them into findings. They are real, they produced the README's best finding, and they are the
**thinnest-tested and least-connected module in the package**.

**Is the finder deep?** Yes as a generator, no as a finder. `nameproof/generate.py` (418 lines)
ships four techniques, `nameproof/gold.py` adds a fifth pass with a stricter gate, all
deterministic and offline. What does not exist is any wire between the two halves: **the
generator has never read the corpus**, and three of its shaping constants say so in their own
comments.

## 1. What exists

| capability | where | verdict |
|---|---|---|

| availability, 6 surfaces, RDAP direct | `nameproof/availability.py`, 211 lines | solid |

| gold reserve names | `nameproof/gold.py`, 115 lines | solid, 15 tests |
| live ground-truth harness | `nameproof/doctor.py` + `corpora/calibration.jsonl` | solid, 12 cases |

Test suite before this session: **60 passed** (`python3 -m pytest tests/ -q`).

### The stats that exist

`CorpusReport` measures, per corpus: single-word share, category-word share, vowel-final share,
syllable distribution of the first word, and single-word length min/median/max. `corpus.fits()`
converts three of those into `OFF_PATTERN` findings for `score --market`.

This is what produced the README's flagship finding: SOC 2 compliance sold as a product names
the category 10% of the time (1/10), investment adviser compliance sold as a service does it 50%
of the time (6/12). That finding is genuinely not available from any general rule about names.

## 2. What is missing or weak, measured

### G1. The generator has never read the corpus. This is the gap.

`generate` and `gold` accept no `--market`. `corpus.py` is imported by `cli.py` for `score` and
for nothing else. The two halves of the tool have never been connected, and the cost is
measurable: three of the generator's shaping constants are hard-coded from a **4-name
cherry-pick** (Vanta, Drata, Sprinto, Alessa), and each one says in its own comment that it is
"chosen, not measured", while the corpus that could measure it sits in the same repository.

Measured this session, generator output at n=200 per technique against every bundled corpus:

| source | ends on a vowel | n |
|---|---|---|
| generator `roots` | **56%** | 200 |
| generator `phonotactic` | **46%** | 200 |
| generator `markov` | 22% | 200 |
| generator `rare` | 12% | 200 |
| corpus soc2-compliance | 30% | 10 |
| corpus regtech-product | 21% | 19 |
| corpus aml-fincrime | 10% | 10 |
| corpus ria-compliance | 8% | 12 |
| corpus dev-cli | **0%** | 12 |

The two techniques a naming pass leans on hardest are calibrated **above every market in the
repository**, and for a CLI-tool market they are calibrated at the wrong end of the scale
entirely. The generator is not wrong, it is uncalibrated: it produces one register regardless of
who the buyer is.

### G2. `corpus.py` carries a shipped substring bug, of the exact class `doctor` exists to catch

`CATEGORY_HINTS` is matched with a plain `in` against the lowercased name. Short hints therefore
fire inside unrelated words. Probed against 40 real startup names:

```
Rapid7     -> ['api']     (r-API-d)
Anthropic  -> ['hr']      (ant-HR-opic)
Threads    -> ['hr']      (t-HR-eads)
```

This is the `normfin` / `normfinder` bug from `calibration.jsonl`, shipped a second time in a
different module. It does not currently corrupt a bundled corpus, which is exactly why it went
unnoticed: it returns a confident, plausible, wrong answer only on names nobody has fed it yet.

### G3. `corpus.py` is the only module in the package with no test file

`tests/` holds `test_phonetics`, `test_generate`, `test_gold`, `test_search`, `test_seo`,
`test_doctor`. There is no `test_corpus.py`. The module Dylan is asking about is the one module
whose output nothing verifies, and the README's headline product-vs-service finding is not
guarded by anything.

### G4. The stats miss the axis the naming rule actually turns on

The skill's own rule of 2026-08-25 says the target is a name that is gold-shaped **and**
meaning-carrying, justified by the measurement that real-word golds are squatted at ~100% while
coined meaning-carriers stay findable. `corpus.py` does not measure **real word vs coined**
anywhere. The single most decision-relevant property of a market's names is the one the stats
module cannot report.

Also absent: the phonetic bar of the market (the corpus is never run through the tool's own
`phonetics.analyse`), and any statement of `n` in the `read as:` verdict line, so a share
computed on 10 names reads exactly like one computed on 1000.

### G5. No cross-corpus comparison, so the flagship finding was made by hand

`market` takes exactly one file. The 10%-versus-50% comparison in the README was produced by a
human running the command twice and diffing the output by eye. The tool cannot state its own
best finding.

### G6. Corpus coverage, and freshness

Six corpora, all compliance/fintech-adjacent, largest is 19 names:
`regtech-product` (19), `dev-cli` (12), `ria-compliance` (12), `soc2-compliance` (10),
`aml-fincrime` (10), plus `roots-trust` (a root lexicon, not a name corpus). Dylan named fintech,
devtools and AI: **AI is absent entirely**, fintech is present only as financial-crime vendors,
devtools only as 12 CLI tools. No corpus carries a source or a date read, so freshness is
unknowable by inspection.

### G7. Dead code

`CorpusReport.initials` is computed on every report and never read, rendered, or tested.

## 3. Ranked plan

Priority follows Dylan's own ordering (stats first, then feed them into generation), corrected by
what the audit found: the stats mostly exist, so the work is deepening them on the one axis that
matters and then wiring them in.

1. **G2 + G3**: fix the substring bug, add `tests/test_corpus.py`, and guard the README's
   product-versus-service finding as a ground-truth test on the real corpora.
2. **G4**: add real-word-versus-coined share, the market's phonetic bar, and `n` in the verdict.
3. **G1**: `--market` on `generate` and `gold`. The corpus drives the vowel-final rate, the
   syllable target and the length band at GENERATION time, and `corpus.fits()` findings enter the
   ranking, so the finder proposes names shaped like the ones that already won in that market.
4. **G5**: `market` takes several files and prints the comparison itself.
5. **G6**: add an AI corpus and a fintech corpus, each with source and date read.
6. **G7**: delete `initials`.

Out of scope, stated so it is a decision rather than an omission: growing the corpora to n>100
each. Every percentage in this tool is currently computed on n between 10 and 19, and that is the
real limit on the stats' authority. Fixing it means a harvest, not a code change.

## 4. What this session actually shipped

Ranked plan items 1 through 6 are done. Test suite: **60 passed before, 93 after**, none removed.

**1. The substring bug is closed, and it was not hypothetical.** `corpus.names_category` now
matches a hint at the start or the end of a token, with a camel-case seam counting as a token
boundary (`MyComplianceOffice` -> my/compliance/office). The proof that it mattered arrived the
same session: the new `corpora/ai-infra.txt` is the first bundled corpus that would have
triggered it, on **2 of its 26 names**, reporting `Anthropic` (ant-**hr**-opic) and `Chroma`
(c-**hr**-oma) as naming the category. An 8% category share for a market whose real share is 0%.

**2. `tests/test_corpus.py` exists**, 25 cases. The README's product-versus-service finding is
now a regression test against the real corpora rather than a claim in prose.

**3. The stats grew the axis the naming rule turns on.** `built from real words` (via
`seo.is_real_word`, shared rather than reimplemented), the market's own median phonetic penalty,
and `n` inside the `read as:` line. When the machine has no system dictionary the real-word row
says it was not measured instead of printing a share off a 52-word stub.

**4. The generator reads the corpus.** `generate --market` and `gold --market` derive a
`corpus.MarketProfile` and use it at GENERATION time: the vowel-final rate, the syllable
distribution and the length band all come from the corpus instead of from constants, and
`corpus.fits` findings enter the ranking. Measured after the wire, `phonotactic` at n=40:

| market | corpus says vowel-final | generator produced |
|---|---|---|
| dev-cli | 0% | **0%** (was 46%) |
| soc2-compliance | 30% | **30%** (was 46%) |
| ria-compliance | 8% | **10%** (was 46%) |

The one-syllable register is reachable for the first time: `dev-cli` is 7 of 12 one-syllable and
the builder previously drew only from {2, 3}, so no seed and no count could produce that market.
Sample output shifted from `Boype, Pipaw, Triduda, Moomaho` to `Bles, Mod, Pal, Rang, Swyd`.

`latin_roots` has a stated limit rather than a hidden one: its vocabulary is 20 roots by 15
suffixes, so a market's register is a pool of a few dozen to a few hundred distinct names.
Against the RIA corpus, `--count 20` realises 7.1% vowel-final across 40 seeds against an 8.3%
target, while `--count 150` drifts to 21% because the closed pool (136 combinations) is
exhausted. Ask for more names than a register holds and you get the other register; the fix is
more roots, not a bigger count.

**5. `market` takes several files** and prints the comparison itself, so the tool can state its
own headline finding.

**6. Two corpora added**, both with source and date read: `ai-infra.txt` (26) and
`fintech-infra.txt` (24). They produce a new finding on their own: AI infrastructure and fintech
infrastructure are both **50% built from real words** against **17%** for developer CLI tools,
and all three name the category **0%** of the time.

**7. `initials` deleted.**

Still out of scope, and still the real limit on the stats' authority: every corpus here is
between 10 and 26 names.

## 5. Contract

The `nameproof` skill is invoked by name from other sessions. Everything above is additive:
new optional flags, new data files, no change to existing output formats or exit codes.
